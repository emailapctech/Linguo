Linguo — home of Signlator
One person signs into a phone camera. The signs become text on the other person's phone.
No special hardware. No gloves. Just the camera every phone already has.
What exists today
Signlator (v1) — a working ASL fingerspelling translator built by Dave Lake on Emergent:
the camera watches your hand, recognizes ASL alphabet letters in real time, and shows them as text, with a live confidence reading.
Live preview: https://signlator-2.preview.emergentagent.com/?utm_source=share
Verified working Nov 16, 2026 — live-detected the letter "C" at 70% confidence on camera.
Roadmap
Stage 1 — hand-tracking proof, refined (v1 exists; being brought home to this repo)
Stage 2 — fingerspelling A–Z → text on the same phone
Stage 3 — two-phone relay: text lands on the other person's phone
Stage 4 — a starter vocabulary of common signs, grown with feedback from Deaf users
Honest scope: fingerspelling and a starter vocabulary are buildable now. Full flowing ASL sentences are still a research frontier — Linguo grows in stages and never oversells.
Known issues
#1 — Lighting. Recognition suffers when the light on the signer's hand is poor or uneven. Planned fix: the app measures light on the hand and coaches live ("face a window") instead of only listing tips.
